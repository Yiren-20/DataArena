Abstract

Abstract

Human egocentric videos offer a scalable source of experience for World Action Models (WAMs), but their value for robot learning depends on more than data volume or video prediction quality. DataArena investigates how data quality, visual diversity, and dataset composition relate to downstream WAM performance. Our framework combines agent-assisted annotation assessment, visual distribution analysis, and controlled pretraining and robot post-training experiments. Initial FastWAM experiments with EgoDex and MicroAGI show a positive relationship between visual data diversity and downstream action-learning performance: the more diverse MicroAGI dataset yields higher robot task success than EgoDex when transferring the pretrained action expert. Transfer outcomes also depend on which model components are initialized from human-video pretraining: transferring both the video and action experts reduces robot task success. Lower zero-shot video denoising loss does not consistently translate into better downstream control in this setting. These observations motivate evaluating human video data through its effects on both video and action learning. These initial findings support the benefits of data diversity for action learning within the evaluated setting. Ongoing work examines how this relationship extends across broader datasets and training conditions, and how annotation quality further affects transfer utility.

The current downstream findings are based on two datasets and one training seed per condition. DataArena's proposed quality and distribution metrics are not yet validated as general predictors of robot success.

MODULE 01 · AGENTIC DATA HEALTH CHECK

Annotation quality and temporal consistency

Screen existing hand-pose annotations for jumps, discontinuities and physically inconsistent motion. Verify candidate anomalies using exactly five consecutive video frames centered on the suspect frame. Refine the verifier with a small budget of human feedback rather than replacing the annotation pipeline.

Evaluation plan: human-verified held-out labels, detection precision/recall/F1, verification ablations and human review effort. Quantitative module results are pending.
MODULE 02 · VISUAL DISTRIBUTION

Diversity, coverage and domain gap

Study visual feature distributions with embeddings, clustering and density estimates. Compare global diversity with target-conditioned coverage: a visually broad dataset need not cover the grasping, contacts or viewpoints that a robot task requires.

Evaluation plan: embedding distribution maps, coverage and redundancy indicators, cross-dataset comparisons and links to held-out transfer metrics. Quantitative module results are pending.
MODULE 03 · DOWNSTREAM TRAINING

Controlled FastWAM transfer validation

Pretrain both the Wan2.2 5B video expert and ActionDiT 1.02B action expert on EgoDex or MicroAGI, with equal training steps and samples. Post-train using 800 RoboTwin 2.0 demonstrations, then compare initializations that transfer the human-pretrained action expert alone or both experts.

Protocol: 60,000 pretraining steps, effective batch 64; 2,000 post-training steps, effective batch 128; 16 robot tasks, 10 rollouts each. In the action-only transfer condition, the video expert is initialized from the original model but is still trained during post-training.
Key observation

Better video prediction does not necessarily imply better robot control.

MicroAGI pretraining improves zero-shot video denoising loss on RoboTwin, yet transferring its pretrained video expert produces a substantial drop in downstream robot success. Transfer utility depends on which expert receives the human-data initialization.

Finding 1 · Zero-shot loss can mislead.

A lower video-denoising loss before adaptation does not guarantee higher robot task success after fine-tuning.

Finding 2 · Expert selection matters.

Action-only transfer avoids the severe degradation observed with full-expert transfer, although its gains over baseline are not statistically established.

Finding 3 · The same data can affect experts differently.

MicroAGI has the highest observed action-only success (70.6%) but the lowest both-expert success (36.2%) among the evaluated settings.

Discussion and interpretation

What explains the transfer gap?

The observed outcomes are clear for this controlled setup, but the mechanism is not yet established. Human-to-robot camera shift, changes in action-relevant features, and rapid task-format adaptation of the original video expert are plausible hypotheses to test.

Hypothesis A

Format adaptation

The original Wan2.2 model may adapt quickly to the prediction format during early post-training, reducing the value of initial zero-shot video performance.

Not yet isolated
Hypothesis B

Embodiment and viewpoint gap

Single-view human video pretraining may bias representations when transferred to multi-camera robot observations.

Not yet isolated
Hypothesis C

Action-relevant representations

Video denoising quality may reward scene statistics without preserving the features most important for action control.

Not yet isolated

Connecting Modules 1–3

Initial comparisons show a positive relationship between visual diversity and downstream action-learning performance: the more diverse MicroAGI dataset outperforms EgoDex under action-only transfer. Ongoing work examines this relationship across additional datasets and controlled diversity levels, alongside the effects of annotation quality. Both positive relationships and negative transfer are meaningful benchmark outcomes.

Current limitations: only EgoDex and MicroAGI; one random seed per training condition; 16 clean-scene tasks with 10 rollouts each; one small-data post-training budget. These results should not be generalized across architectures or training regimes without additional evidence.
